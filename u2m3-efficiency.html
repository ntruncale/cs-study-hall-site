The boy who added 1 to 100

Context

There's a famous story, probably improved over the years, about the mathematician Carl Friedrich Gauss as a schoolboy in Germany in the 1780s. His teacher wanted some peace and quiet, so he told the class to add up every number from 1 to 100.

Most students started grinding away: 1 + 2 = 3, 3 + 3 = 6, 6 + 4 = 10… Gauss wrote one number on his slate almost immediately. He had noticed that 1 + 100 = 101, 2 + 99 = 101, 3 + 98 = 101, and so on. There are 50 of those pairs, so the total is 50 × 101 = 5,050.

His classmates' method needed 99 additions. His needed one multiplication. Both are correct, but one is far more efficient. Thinking about efficiency is how computer scientists make programs that finish in seconds instead of years.

2

Three ideas

Experience

Idea 1: Count steps, not seconds

Computers keep getting faster, so timing a program doesn't tell you much. Instead, computer scientists ask: as the input gets bigger, how fast does the number of steps grow? That's what efficiency means.

Idea 2: Three common growth patterns

  • Slow growth (logarithmic): binary search. Double the list and you add just one more step.
  • Steady growth (linear): linear search, or reading every name aloud. Double the list and the steps double.
  • Fast growth (quadratic): comparing every item with every other item. Double the list and the steps go up about four times.

Idea 3: Some problems explode

Imagine planning a delivery route by trying every possible order of stops. With 5 stops there are 120 orders. With 10 stops, 3,628,800. With 20 stops, about 2.4 quintillion. Even the fastest computer can't try them all, so real route-planning apps use clever shortcuts that find a very good route quickly instead of a perfect one slowly.

Reflection

Efficiency is a gift when it means the pantry van saves gas or a program finishes in seconds. But some things shouldn't be rushed or optimized, like listening to a friend who's struggling, or praying. Jesuits call this kind of attention cura personalis, care for the whole person.
