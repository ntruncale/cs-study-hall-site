A cannonball and a choice

Context

In 1521, a young Spanish soldier named Íñigo López de Loyola was badly wounded by a cannonball while defending the city of Pamplona. During months of recovery, the only books he could find were a life of Christ and stories of the saints.

Lying in bed, he noticed something. Daydreams about fame and adventure excited him for a while, then left him feeling empty. Imagining a life like the saints' left him with a peace that lasted. Paying attention to those feelings changed his life. He became St. Ignatius, founded the Jesuits, and taught people a way of making good decisions called discernment.

Computers make decisions too, but in a much simpler way: they check whether something is true or false, and then do one thing or another. Today you'll teach your programs to make those decisions.

Three ideas

Experience

Idea 1: input() asks the user a question

input() shows a question, waits for the user to type an answer, and gives you the answer as text. To do math or compare numbers, turn the text into a whole number with int().

Code
name = input("What's your name? ")
age = int(input("How old are you? "))
print(f"{name}, next year you'll be {age + 1}.")
Output (the user typed Ana and 15)
What's your name? Ana
How old are you? 15
Ana, next year you'll be 16.

Idea 2: Comparisons are True or False

A comparison checks two values and gives back True or False.

SymbolMeansExample that's True
==equal to5 == 5
!=not equal to5 != 3
< >less than, greater than3 < 5
<= >=at most, at least5 >= 5
Watch out: one equals sign = stores a value. Two equals signs == compare values.

Idea 3: if, elif, else

if runs some lines only when a comparison is True. elif ("else if") checks another comparison. else runs when nothing above it was True. Python checks from the top and runs only the first match.

Two rules: the if, elif and else lines end with a colon, and the lines that belong to them are indented 4 spaces.

Code
temp = int(input("Temperature? "))
if temp >= 85:
    print("Bring water to the service site.")
elif temp >= 60:
    print("Perfect weather for the park cleanup.")
else:
    print("Wear a jacket!")
Output (the user typed 72)
Temperature? 72
Perfect weather for the park cleanup.
