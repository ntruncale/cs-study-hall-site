User No. 4417749

Context

In August 2006, the internet company AOL published about 20 million web searches made by around 650,000 of its users, to help researchers. To protect privacy, AOL replaced each user's name with a number. The data was supposed to be anonymous.

Reporters from The New York Times looked at the searches made by user No. 4417749: searches for landscapers in a town called Lilburn, Georgia, for people with the last name Arnold, and for "dog that urinates on everything." Within days they found and interviewed the searcher: Thelma Arnold, a 62-year-old widow in Lilburn. She was shocked. "My goodness, it's my whole personal life," she said.

AOL apologized and took the data down, but copies had already spread. No single search identified her. Put together, they pointed to exactly one person.

2

Three ideas

Experience

Idea 1: Your footprint has two parts

Your digital footprint is the trail of data you leave online. Your active footprint is what you choose to share: posts, photos, comments, your bio. Your passive footprint is what gets collected without you typing anything: your location, which videos you watch and for how long, which websites you visit.

Idea 2: Small details add up

Like Thelma Arnold's searches, details that seem harmless on their own can be combined to identify you or reveal private things. Computer scientist Latanya Sweeney showed in 2000 that about 87% of Americans can be uniquely identified by just three facts: their ZIP code, birth date and sex.

Idea 3: How to protect yourself and others

  • Permissions: give an app only what it needs to do its job. A flashlight doesn't need your contacts.
  • Privacy settings: choose who can see your posts, find you and tag you.
  • Think before you post: your address, schedule, full birthday and pet's name (a common security question) are valuable to strangers and scammers.
  • Watch for phishing: messages that pretend to be someone you trust in order to steal your password or information. They often create panic ("your account will be deleted!") or offer something too good to be true.
  • Respect others: ask before you post a photo of someone or share their story.
