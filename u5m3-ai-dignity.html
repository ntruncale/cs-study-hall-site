A new Pope, an old name, a new revolution

Context

In 1891, during the Industrial Revolution, Pope Leo XIII wrote a letter called Rerum Novarum ("Of New Things"). Factories and machines were changing work and family life, and he defended the dignity of workers.

In May 2025, a few days after he was elected, Pope Leo XIV explained that he chose his name partly because of that history. The Church, he said, faces another industrial revolution, this time driven by artificial intelligence, with new challenges for human dignity, justice and work.

Earlier that year, in January 2025, the Vatican published Antiqua et Nova ("Ancient and New"), a document about how AI relates to human intelligence. It describes AI as a remarkable product of human creativity, and it insists that AI must serve people, never the other way around.

2

Three ideas

Experience

Idea 1: What AI does, and doesn't do

Tools like chatbots and image generators are trained on huge amounts of text and pictures. They produce new text or images by predicting what's likely to come next, based on patterns in that data. They can be impressively useful. But they don't understand the way you do, they can't love or take responsibility, and they sometimes state false things with total confidence. These made-up answers are often called hallucinations. AI can also create deepfakes: realistic fake images, video or audio of real people.

Idea 2: What Antiqua et Nova teaches (in summary)

  • Human intelligence is more than calculation. It belongs to a whole person, with a body, relationships, a conscience and a longing for truth, goodness and God.
  • AI is a tool. It can imitate some things intelligence does, but it should support human thinking and work, not replace it.
  • People stay responsible. Humans, not machines, are morally accountable for how AI is designed and used.
  • AI should serve human dignity and the common good, especially the poor and vulnerable. The document raises concerns about misinformation, privacy, jobs, education, relationships, weapons and the environment.

Idea 3: Discernment, one tool at a time

St. Ignatius taught people to notice where their choices lead: toward life, peace and love, or away from them. You can ask the same questions about any use of AI:

Is it honest? Would I be comfortable telling my teacher or family exactly how I used it?
Does it help me grow, or does it do the growing for me?
Could it harm someone, their privacy, their reputation or their dignity?
Does it bring me closer to people, or pull me away from them?
