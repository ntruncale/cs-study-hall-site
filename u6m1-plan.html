An 11-year-old and a city's water

Context

When Gitanjali Rao was about 10 years old, she watched news coverage of the water crisis in Flint, Michigan, where families had been drinking water contaminated with lead. Testing water for lead was slow and expensive, and she kept wondering whether there was a better way.

She spent months researching and, at age 11, designed a small device she called Tethys that uses special sensors and a phone app to detect lead in water quickly. It won a national young scientist competition in 2017. Later she built Kindly, an app that uses AI to help catch cyberbullying before a message is sent. In 2020, TIME named her its first-ever Kid of the Year.

She didn't start with technology. She started with people who needed help, and then asked what she could build. That's exactly what you'll do today.

How designers plan

Experience

Idea 1: Design thinking starts with people

Many designers use a process called design thinking:

1. EmpathizeGet to know the person. What is their day like? What's hard for them?
2. DefineName the problem clearly in one sentence.
3. ImagineBrainstorm several ideas before choosing one.
4. Plan & buildPlan the program, then build it a step at a time.
5. TestHave the person try it, listen, and improve it.

Idea 2: A good problem statement

Use this pattern: "[Person] needs a way to [do something] because [reason]." For example: "My grandmother needs a way to remember which pills to take each morning because she has four different prescriptions."

Idea 3: Keep it small enough to finish

You'll have one study hall to build and one to test and share. Pick something you can make with the tools you know: input(), if, for and functions. A small program that really works beats a giant one that never runs.
